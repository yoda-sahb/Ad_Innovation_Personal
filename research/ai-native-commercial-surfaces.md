# AI-Native Commercial Surfaces

[Portfolio](../README.md) · [Research method](../docs/research-method.md)

**Status:** Exploratory hypothesis. Evaluation below is proposed; no user-study or commercial results are published here.

> How can commercial participation enter a decision process without compromising the reasoning that makes an assistant useful?

## Problem and intended value

A conversational assistant can help a user understand a need, compare choices, and act. Introducing paid participation into that process raises questions beyond ad placement: relevance, disclosure, influence on the answer, and responsibility for commercial claims.

The intended value is useful commercial discovery with a clear boundary between paid participation and the assistant's underlying assistance.

## Working hypothesis

Commercial options may be useful within an AI conversation if their role is explicit, their claims can be assessed, and payment does not distort the organic reasoning provided to the user.

Whether such a boundary can be maintained in practice remains an open question.

## Illustrative scenario

A user asks for help choosing a product. The assistant first establishes the user's needs and comparison criteria. A sponsored option may then appear with clear disclosure and the same suitability checks applied to other options. If no paid option fits, commercial participation should not force a recommendation.

This is a proposed interaction to evaluate, not a claim about any existing assistant.

## Assumptions to challenge

- Commercial intent can be assessed without unnecessary disclosure of conversational content.
- Users can distinguish paid participation from independent assistance.
- Product claims, availability, and suitability can be checked sufficiently for the task.
- Commercial incentives can be prevented from changing the reasoning or concealing better options.

## Proposed evaluation

Compare an organic-only experience with an explicitly disclosed commercial experience on matched tasks. Establish acceptable limits for harm before interpreting revenue or engagement gains.

| Question | Evidence to examine |
| --- | --- |
| Is the experience useful? | Task completion, suitability, and quality of the comparison |
| Do users understand sponsorship? | Disclosure comprehension and perceived independence |
| Does payment alter reasoning? | Matched scenarios varying sponsorship while holding user needs and product facts constant |
| Are commercial claims dependable? | Accuracy of product facts and handling of uncertainty or unavailable information |
| Are boundaries respected? | Unnecessary data disclosure and behavior when no sponsored option fits |

Clicks, purchases, or revenue alone would not establish that the interaction preserves user trust or decision quality.

## Evidence that would weaken the hypothesis

Users consistently misunderstand the commercial role; payment changes advice in ways unrelated to user needs; or maintaining the boundary removes the economic incentive to participate.

## Open questions

- What separates helpful discovery from commercial steering?
- How should the assistant respond to unverified product claims?
- Who is responsible when price, availability, or suitability is wrong?
- When should the assistant omit commercial participation altogether?
